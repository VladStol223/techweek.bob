What the Engineering Audit Skill Does (9 Stages)

A single /engineering-audit command kicks off a structured 9-stage workflow that Bob runs autonomously. No templates to fill in. No checklist to follow manually. Bob does the work and reports findings in a structured format you can act on immediately.

The 9 Audit Stages Bob Runs Autonomously
Stage 1 — Repository Discovery
  Maps every layer: frontend, backend, DB, auth, tests, CI/CD, secrets.
  Produces a full Architecture Map before touching source code.

Stage 2 — Technical Audit
  Opens source files. Classifies every finding: Critical, High, Medium, Low, 
  False Positive, or Product Decision Required.
  Every finding includes: location, steps to reproduce, root cause, fix, effort.

Stage 3 — Product Decision Gate
  Any finding that requires a business decision is escalated here.
  Bob presents options, tradeoffs, and a recommendation. Waits for your input.

Stage 4 — Remediation
  Implements the smallest safe fix for every Critical and High finding.
  Adds regression tests where feasible. Marks each finding [FIXED].

Stage 5 — Independent Verification
  Acts as a new auditor who has never seen the code.
  Re-opens every file. Verifies each fix with a source-code excerpt.

Stage 6 — Validation
  Runs: type check, lint, production build, unit tests, integration tests.
  Reports each check as [PASS], [FAIL], or [SKIP] with explicit reason.

Stage 7 — First-Time User Journey Audit
  Traces every user flow as a new user who has never seen the product.
  Identifies dead ends, false success messages, missing recovery paths.

Stage 8 — Beta Readiness Triage
  Classifies every open finding: Must Fix / Should Fix / Post-Beta / Known Dependency.
  Gives a recommended fix sequence.

Stage 9 — Release Report
  Produces a complete release report.
  Concludes with: Go / Go With Conditions / No-Go and the reasoning.

Run Your Own Competitive Research Sprint

Use the prompts below to run your own version of the ShellyOS research sprint. You bring the vision. Bob handles the analysis. The first prompt gives you a deep competitive landscape. The second turns the intelligence into a strategic architecture.

Prompt 1 — Competitive Intelligence Sprint
I'm building [describe your startup / product idea in 2-3 sentences].

Act as a senior competitive intelligence analyst. Research the landscape of existing solutions:
1. Identify the 10-15 most relevant open-source or commercial alternatives
2. For each: what it does, what stack it uses, what it does well, where the gaps are
3. Map each alternative to my concept: direct competitor / adjacent / potential component
4. Identify the 3 biggest white spaces no existing solution covers well
5. Synthesize: what does the existence of this landscape tell me about what to build?

Be specific. Cite real projects with real capabilities. Do not give me a generic market overview.
Prompt 2 — Turn Intelligence Into Architecture
Based on the competitive analysis above, help me design the architecture for [your product].

Given the landscape, produce:
1. CORE DIFFERENTIATION STATEMENT — What I build that nothing else does
2. CAPABILITY MAP — The 10-15 core capabilities my product needs, ranked by strategic importance
3. BUILD vs INTEGRATE DECISION — For each capability: build from scratch / use open-source component / license commercial
4. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE BRIEF — The recommended stack, data model, and integration approach
5. SPRINT 1 TASK LIST — The first 2 weeks of building, broken into concrete executable tasks

Ground every recommendation in the competitive landscape you just analyzed.
